# Tradewinds

A dashboard and trading bot for [Tradewinds](https://tradewinds.kibb.io/), the headless Age of Sail trading game played over an HTTP API. Sign in, found or pick a company, watch your ships, trade at port order books, and let the fleet autopilot run your ships, pick lanes and buy more ships for you.

Talks to `https://tradewinds.kibb.io/v1` (game guide: `/skill.md`, saved as [docs/skill.md](docs/skill.md); spec: `/api-docs/openapi.json`, saved as [docs/kibb-openapi.json](docs/kibb-openapi.json)). After re-downloading the spec, `node scripts/gen-kibb-schema.mjs` regenerates `src/lib/kibb/schema.ts` from it.

## Tech Stack

- **Next.js 15** with React 19 and TypeScript
- **Tailwind CSS 4** for styling
- **Base UI** for unstyled, accessible components
- **pnpm** as the package manager

## Getting Started

```bash
pnpm install
pnpm dev
```

Open [http://localhost:3000](http://localhost:3000), register or sign in, then found or pick a company. That's the whole setup: no database, no env vars.

- Your passphrase goes straight to kibb.io and is never stored. Sign-in gets a 90-day player key, kept in an httpOnly cookie.
- Picking a company mints a company-bound API key, saved server-side in `.kibb/store.json` (gitignored) so the autopilot can trade while no browser is open.
- Logins are rate limited (12 a minute), so don't hammer the sign-in form.

| Variable | Description | Default |
|---|---|---|
| `KIBB_API_URL` | Game API base URL | `https://tradewinds.kibb.io` |
| `KIBB_STORE_DIR` | Where keys and autopilot settings are kept | `.kibb/` |
| `TOPSTATS_API_KEY` | TopStats token for naming new ships after Discord bots | bundled names |
| `COOKIE_SECURE` | Force the session cookies' `Secure` flag (`true`/`false`) | on when the request came over HTTPS |

## Fleet autopilot

Runs inside the Next.js server; start and stop it from the dashboard. It puts every ship under its own agent and keeps them busy:

- **Lanes.** A lane is one leg of work: sail to a source, load, sail to a destination. Three kinds: **charter** (goods for charters there, live ones and gossiped ones, counted at the chance they're still open; a Crown or Admiralty charter's premium over the goods' value counted after the taper, guide 3.7: once our Crown and Admiralty charter pay over the last 10 game days passes 50,000 Cr, all our companies together and the load's own pay included, the premium above the base price (or the pay, if lower) is multiplied by √(50,000 Cr ÷ earnings); players' charters pay as posted, and expansion tons still count in full for the completion bonus; the panel and the AI digest show the earnings and the factor), **market** (goods sold to the destination's buyers at the last bid seen there, marked down for its age), and **freight** (Merchant Adventurers lots at the ship's own port). Spare hold and cash are filled with other goods the source sells that the destination wants, and freight lots for the same destination. Lawful trade only (guide 3.18): contraband (catalog category `contraband`) is never bought, sold, carried for a charter or valued for a lawful sale, and a smugglers' cove (`is_cove`) is never a source, destination or buyer of a lane, nor a factor's port, nor counted as quay presence for ship listings and envelopes. A ship with contraband aboard is never offered on a market and never berths, buys stores, loads, refits or takes freight in an ordinary port (a ship lying there with it may be searched, berthed more than in the roadstead; a free berth takes her on arrival, and she leaves it the moment its lock lets her); unless the smuggling mode takes her (below), she holds it in the roadstead with a log line saying so. Costs: the ask with price impact, customs (the live figure from the last book or port view), the remittance fee, berth dues for the stay loading at the source and the one at the destination (the port's posted daily rate, 10 Cr a day until one is seen, at least 6 game hours a stay), and the cargo pirates are expected to take (live zone piracy, net of marques, patrols and the ship's guns; pirates go for the richest prizes, so a valuable load draws more raids and an empty ship almost none). The chance of pirates taking the ship itself is charged to the leg at the hull's replacement value. Storms are charged the same way. Each leg rolls for the zone it ends in at the catalog's storm odds per game day at sea, judged against the leg's water (the rougher of that zone and the crossing itself, guide 3.5: a hull below it fares worse), worse in storm season (game months 11-2, about 2 real days in every 6). A hit destroys a share of every stack and of the stores (most storms are light, a heavy one takes much more; the share is learned from `cargo_loss_pct`), and a rare sinking costs the hull. Lanes through long, stormy legs (Baltic, Dogger Bank, Norwegian Trench) rank lower then, and Channel and short legs win. How often storms really strike is learned from the fleet's own voyages: each passage's exposure against the storms met, starting at half the zone's rate, doubled in storm season. On first start the fleet reads the company's 7-day event log for this, and the dashboard shows the figures. The game doesn't publish these formulas: an interception takes the ship itself in 5-10% of cases (fewer the more guns face it), else seizes 20-50% of every stack and of the stores; the share a raid takes (35% to start) and the capture odds (7.5% unarmed to start) are learned from the fleet's own raids within those ranges. Stores are stocked for the hits a passage could plausibly take: the route's safe figure covers one storm only (the worst), each further storm is planned to take 40% of them until five storms have shown the fleet's own `stores_loss_pct` (then the 90th percentile of those, never under 25%), and a pirate raid at worst half. Lanes are ranked by profit per hour over the leg plus the best leg visible from the destination, so a lane ending where a paid return cargo waits beats a dead end. Loads are sized to the cash the fleet can spend when the ship reaches the source (see Cash); reservations spread ships across charters, sources, buyers and freight.
- **Phase bonus.** Expansion charters also earn a share of the tier's completion bonus (12% of the tier's base value), split by the square root of each player's tons valued at base prices, a player's companies pooled: a ton of cannons counts for far more than a ton of provisions, and the first tons earn the most. A load is credited with what it adds to our player's share, pool × [√(ours+t)/(√(ours+t)+Σ√others) − √ours/(√ours+Σ√others)], with ours our companies' tons on the contribution board plus what our other ships are already carrying there, each other company as its own player (who owns which isn't public), and everyone's tons grown to the tier's projected total (the pool is paid on the final tons), so a fleet far ahead sees little more bonus and a first delivery a lot. Without a board to split (nobody else on it, our names not known yet) it falls back to the pool's straight rate on base value (clamped to 6-20%). That expected share, discounted by a setting (75% by default), counts in lane choice and buy prices. The dashboard and leaderboard show each company's estimated share of the bonus by the same split.
- **Hold space.** Each gun takes 2 t of hold. With guns on "auto", a ship calling at a shipyard is refitted to the count that earns most on its lane, weighing cargo room against the cargo pirates are expected to take there and the risk of losing the ship to them. In phase-1 waters that is usually none. Surplus stores above what the voyage needs are moved out and sold when the hold is what limits a load.
- **Events.** It long-polls the company's event log: a ship's agent wakes the moment it arrives, is berthed from the queue, is evicted or hit by a hazard, and a posted, filled or expired charter refreshes the world within seconds so idle ships re-plan at once. Timers remain the fallback if the feed drops.
- **World events** (setting, on by default). It reads the public world-event feed with every world refresh (once a minute, and within about 5 seconds of a world-event notice on the event log) and keeps the last 11 game days of it. A **shortage** raises the bid expected at that port for that good, and a **glut** lowers the ask there; both fade to nothing by the news' lapse (30 game hours), so lanes form toward shortages and from gluts while a ship can still get there (ports in one are tried on top of the best few buyers and sellers). How big they are is learned from the books our ships read while one is on (1.15× and 0.85× base to start), a book read since it began replaces the guess, and a price read at a shock's height is eased back as the shock fades. Every shortage brings a Governor crisis charter, listed only on its quay or as gossip hours later. Until a listing shows it, lanes count it as expected: the tons the news names (at most 350), at the crisis pay seen so far (1.25× base to start), trusted at the share of crisis charters found when looked for (60% to start), with at most one ship's load on it, less for laden rivals seen heading there; the buyer never builds a ship for one, nor counts one as a lane's onward leg. The real charter replaces it as soon as a listing or one of our ships in port shows it, and a ship already in port lists the quay again once the news is an hour old, so one that isn't there is dropped then; if a load arrives to find none, it is sold at the port's shortage bid or taken to a better market. Admiralty mobilizations are public charters, planned as soon as the listing shows them. A pirate outbreak counts in the zone's piracy until the zones are read again. A structural shift re-reads the catalog (what each port makes and imports; live customs are re-read every 30 minutes anyway), and asks from before a production collapse, or bids from before an import slump, at that port are no longer planned on. Off: no shortage or glut prices and no expected crisis charters; outbreaks, structural shifts and finding crisis charters still count. The dashboard lists the events in force, what the planner does with each, and the learned figures.
- **Sailing.** Buys up to the charter's break-even price, keeps stores at the route's safe figure (which survives the worst storm on one leg) or more on passages likely to take two or three (long ones, and in storm season), refuses water the hull isn't rated for (lane *and* sea-zone class), delivers from the roadstead when the per-ton berth lock would cost more than waiting out the base lock (several charters in one batch call), and times everything on the server's clock. A leg the server expects to starve, stores running out at sea, or a mutiny anywhere in the fleet adds a game day of stores kept in hand (up to 6; safe arrivals wear it down). Orders never match our own other companies: their resting asks and bids, found when an order of ours crosses them and they stay untouched, are left out of the prices from then on. Coffee-house gossip gives each port's best players' resting bid and ask with their tons: a lane may sell into a remote player's bid above the port's buyers, and buy from a remote player's ask below the ladder, up to those tons (half gone per game day of the news' age); our own resting orders (`market/orders`, read every 10 minutes; the fleet itself trades only IOC) are left out, and with other companies of yours in the game the gossip's levels aren't counted at all (theirs can't be told apart, and orders never match them). A ship arrested for a defaulted loan (a `409` on departing or joining a convoy that says so, or the default's notice) waits in the roadstead and buys nothing until our loans show it free, re-checked every 5 minutes.
- **Freight.** Picks up Merchant Adventurers freight on legs it sails anyway when the reward beats the berth time it costs. A lot's reward counts only at the chance the ship arrives, and a sinking or a capture forfeits its collateral too, so freight over dangerous water is priced at that risk.
- **Passengers** (setting, on by default; guide 3.20). Groups wait at ports for a destination, and each game hour every group boards the best value among the open offers (cheaper wins, and so does faster; a group never splits); a passenger takes 1 t of hold (a packet carries 2 a ton). The fare is paid on arrival, cut for every game day late, and lost with the ship (Lloyd's doesn't insure fares; pirates ransom passengers). The autopilot only fills spare hold on voyages it sails anyway, never a detour: once a ship's cargo and stores are aboard for the port she makes for, it offers her free hold to the groups waiting for that port that fit whole, a little under the median fare accepted on the route lately (with fewer than 3 records, the world's fare per sea mile for the passage), less for a slow hull, never under what the weight's extra sailing time costs, nudged up after a group boards and down after an unfilled round. It holds at the berth for one game-hour round only when the fares expected beat the wait. Never on a smuggler, with contraband aboard, arrested, insured or being retired. Passengers aboard pin her next port to theirs, and their fares count as cash due at her arrival. The panel shows fares earned, the groups aboard and our offers; the Passengers page has the waiting groups, the fares board and a manual offer.
- **Convoys** (setting, on by default). Just before a ship sails, it looks for our other ships bound the same way: it joins one of our convoys forming at that port for the same destination, or forms a private one (as flagship, from the roadstead) when another of our ships will be ready to leave the same way within 6 game hours. In a convoy pirates board just one ship and guns are pooled, so each of n ships' expected piracy losses (cargo, and the ship itself) roughly fall by (n-1)/n; it convoys only when that, less the extra storm loss of the slower and later passage, beats what the wait costs the ship's time, and only with ships of similar speed (a convoy sails at its slowest). Safe water and empty passages sail alone, and so does a ship whose port's forming convoys can't all be read. Convoy departures aren't checked for stores, so a ship's stores are checked against the convoy's pace and the wait in port when it joins or forms one, and again on every pass while it waits (a slower ship may join later): short, it moves provisions from its cargo into the stores, or leaves to top up and joins again or sails alone, unless a policy may count on the consorts and the bare need is still covered. While waiting in a convoy a ship does nothing else; it sails with the others at the set time.
- **Your policies.** The autopilot never buys insurance, but a policy you bought for a ship binds its next voyage: it sails for the policy's port with what is aboard and nowhere else (sealed freight that would otherwise miss its deadline wins, logged as voiding the policy), and loads nothing more by any path, purchases, freight or stores moved into the hold (cargo added after a policy is written voids it, `cargo_exceeds_declared`); it reads the policy afresh before every purchase and freight lot, so one you buy mid-load stops the loading where it stands. It never sells at the policy's origin (that cuts the cover), keeps its guns, and stays in a convoy whose consorts the policy may count on. A voided policy's reason is logged. Insure a ship once it is loaded, and declare the whole cargo. The dashboard asks before you detach an insured ship from a convoy.
- **Cash.** Buying is paid from the port's banking region; it remits from other regions on demand (instant, at the live ACH fee) and always keeps the reserve in the home region. A load is planned on the cash that will be spare when the ship reaches its source: cash in hand, less what our other ships' loads will spend first, plus a share (60% by default, setting) of what our ships at sea are due to be paid before then. That covers charter pay and sales at the planned price, and freight reward and collateral, less the remittance fee to the source's region; cargo held for a re-post isn't counted. At the source, just before buying, the load is planned again from there with the cash there is then: bigger when deliveries paid meanwhile, the best smaller mix when the cash went elsewhere (setting, on by default). A ship never spends cash another ship's load counts on, unless cash due lands before that ship buys; a ship at its source keeps first call on the share of its own planned load that was planned on cash in hand (less what a part dearer than planned has already taken), and only the cash due it counted on, or growth beyond its plan, must leave the others' loads alone. A load that counted on cash due not yet landed waits for it in the roadstead rather than sail with less than the planner's minimum load. The log says when a load was re-sized and the panel counts them. A ship waiting for cash tries again as soon as another delivery pays, or a re-sized load frees some.
- **Letters of marque** (off by default). Every 5 minutes it weighs each sea zone: rival cargo passing through over the last game day, from quay sightings and (with rival intel) public insurance policies and convoys, or the sailings already announced for the next day if more (a convoy counts once, at its richest member's cargo), against the cost of a one-day marque and the extra piracy our own ships would face there. The game prices a marque per zone from the last 7 game days of player traffic (60% of the prize money it expects after the Admiral's tenth), so the most promising few zones are quoted (`marque-quote`), each sized to what the budget has left at its price. It sponsors the best one only when there are at least 3 laden rival passages a day and the quoted prize beats the quoted cost plus our own ships' extra losses there (cargo seized, and ships taken), within the budget (1,000 Cr in any 24 hours by default), priced again just before it is issued. Never in a zone a voyage we underwrite sails through (a marque would raise the claims on our own slices), and none at all while our slices can't be read in full. The zone's 5,000 bps marque cap is respected. **Naval patrols** share the switch, the budget and the gates (guide 3.5): the game prices a patrol per zone from its recent smuggling traffic, never under 2 Cr a bps-day, and pays its sponsors half the base value of every smuggler seized at sea in the zone while it is in force, ship and everything aboard, split by bps, less the Admiral's tenth (none for a seizure in port). The autopilot models each zone's prize money from the public `smuggler_seized` notices over the last 7 game days (the pool each is worth learned from our own `patrol_prize_paid`, else half the cheapest hull; with no patrol in force there now, ours finds only its share of the patrol strength they were seized under, read from the first zones read within 2 game hours after each seizure, or of a 1,500-bps patrol when none was: a replayed notice never counts as a full catch) and adds the piracy a patrol takes off our own ships' passages; the zones that pay even at the 2 Cr floor are quoted (`patrol-quote`), and the best is funded for one game day when that beats the quote, priced again just before. Never where a ship of ours carries contraband (a patrol there always loses: its search may take her) or while that can't be told, never where our marque is in force, and no marque where our patrol is. Prize money is booked into the vault it lands in and logged; the rivals page shows each zone's verdict and our patrols' prize.
- **Rival intel** (setting, on by default). Besides sailings at ports where we have presence, it reads the public insurance list (which ship of which company means to sail where, with how much cargo declared; closed policies give arrivals and sinkings), public convoys (schedule, hulls, guns), the coffee houses' convoy news at our quays (organizer, route, sailing, muster: counted as low-confidence traffic only while no listing or quay sighting has that convoy, and never with no members mustered), the expansion board (who delivered how much to which charter: each charter's growth less our own deliveries, named when exactly one rival's contribution grew by that much) and the hourly leaderboard (net worth, so earning rate). Sources are tied together (company id to name, ship id to name) only on unambiguous matches. The planner takes from a charter's room what rivals due there before us are likely to deliver (by their declared cargo or their past deliveries there; laden ones of unknown cargo take a share as before); a laden rival likely to deliver to a stalled expansion charter before it lapses lowers the odds of the richer re-post (lanes count on it less, and below 40% the fleet won't let the posting lapse for it). A laden rival's sailing notice re-runs that within seconds. The underwriter skips an insured whose own sinkings on a lane (8 closed policies at least) exceed Lloyd's bound. About 1.5 extra reads a minute. Off: quay sightings only, as before.
- **Underwriting** (off by default). Every minute (and soon after a new listing) it reads other companies' open policies; each still open for underwriting carries Lloyd's own estimate of its expected claim (an upper bound) and the premium's margin over it. It writes slices where that margin reaches the minimum (150 bps by default), best margins first, taking first pick before Lloyd's looks at a listing an hour on. A listing without the estimate is no longer open for underwriting (sailed, fully covered, expired, or no route) and is never written. A remainder under 500 bps is taken whole. A storm claims the share of the declared cargo it destroyed (it varies, `cargo_loss_pct`), pirates who let the ship go at most what they seized of it (up to half), a sinking or capture the whole slice. Slices are capped per policy (3,000 Cr) and in total escrow (5,000 Cr), paid only from spare cash above each region's floor; policies older than 36 game hours are skipped as likely at sea. Our own companies' slices are read in full (page by page) so none is written on twice; when they can't all be listed, it writes nothing that round. A sinking costs the whole slice, so keep the budget to what you can lose. The panel also shows the Lloyd's List our quays post (Lloyd's and player underwriters' loss ratio and return on cover over its window, and the policies open at each quay), for reference only.
- **Expansion re-posts and stalls.** Expansion charters are re-posted when they lapse (every 30 game days in Phase 2, 12 real hours, all of a tier's at once), so their expiry doesn't end the work: lanes keep planning on them, and the buyer counts on them for as long as the tier lasts at its recent pace. A posting nobody delivers to comes back richer (about +25% pay on 75% of the tons; the autopilot learns the real figures when it sees one), and the higher pay stays for later postings. The guide calls a posting stalled while under a tenth of its expected pace (its tons spread over its window) is delivered, and says any rival delivering cancels the escalation, "so waiting rarely pays". The autopilot judges that per posting: the pace line runs from the posting's listed start (else its lapse less the usual window), and any other company's delivery into it ends the stall for that posting, our own other companies' included (the server counts them as other companies). Whether our own small deliveries cancel it isn't published (the feed has shown a posting with only 1.5% delivered that didn't escalate), so it starts safe: ours are taken to cancel it, and re-posts teach it otherwise. A posting only our ships delivered to, still under the line late in its window, that re-posts richer is a confirmation; after 2 in a row, a ship may deliver into a posting being let lapse when the whole of its load for it keeps it under 80% of the line, and one such re-post that doesn't escalate switches that off again (each is logged and the record is saved). So for each stalled posting the fleet makes one call (setting, on by default): let it lapse when the chance it escalates × the extra pay × the tons it expects to put into the re-post (its recent flow there over a posting, loads already bound for it that can wait for it) is at least 1.25 times what waiting costs (loaded ships landing first or already holding, at their earning rate and the cash they tie up; their tons the smaller re-post can't take, sold at the bid instead; part of the margin on its own flow there until the lapse and on loads it shuts out, those landing more than 4 real hours early), and keep doing so while it covers that cost. The odds come from stalls seen lapse, each charter's rival record (how often rivals broke its stalls, from the public contributor board, pooled with the tier's) and laden rivals seen bound for its port (likelier from a port selling the good or a company seen delivering there). Under 20%, a likely rival delivery first, an AI "deliver" call, the season ending before the re-post, a tier that may complete first, or loads of ours (into any of the tier's postings) that would complete it, or a loaded ship that would wait over 4 real hours: it delivers. While it lets a posting lapse no ship delivers into it (beyond such a load, once learned), the tons a ship's load plans into it that land early wait in the roadstead (never planned more than 4 real hours early; the rest of the good goes where it was planned, and leftovers are never held), ships take other work until a run lands just after the lapse, and holders deliver at once when a rival's public delivery notice shows up. A re-post that stalls again is just another stalled posting. "Always hold" (off by default) and an AI "wait" force it in a posting's last 4 game hours whatever the math says. The dashboard shows each charter's state, the fleet's call with its numbers, rival deliveries seen and the tier's ETA.
- **Scarce cash.** When cash is what limits the fleet (a ship waits for it, or what's spare wouldn't fill half a load), lanes are charged for the money their loads tie up, at half the fleet's own return on cash, so high-margin loads win over thin ones that lock up the same money. The Crown's wealth levy (guide 3.12) is charged on net worth, as the leaderboard counts it (cash, escrow, goods, hulls, loans, deposits and venture stakes): above 1,000,000 Cr a company pays 0.1% of the excess a game day (about 6% a real day), taken from cash, the richest vault first. Putting money to work doesn't avoid it, so it never makes spending free; it is a daily bill like upkeep, and the home floor keeps 1.5 game days of it (on the hourly leaderboard's net worth while fresh, else the fleet's own estimate, or what the ledger's `crown_wealth_levy` entries show was charged if more). The panel shows the excess and the levy it pays. Once the tier is 90% done or due within 2 hours, and for an hour after it turns over, the buyer keeps a share of spare cash back (40% by default) for the next tier's charters, which nobody can reach at first. Hulls, marques and underwriting never count on cash due.
- **Leverage** (setting, on by default; conservative; the key needs `treasury_remit`). Loans are by quote (guide 3.13): a fixed annual rate (bps per 360 game days, simple interest for the game time elapsed) for 1-360 game days, repaid balloon or amortised (installments every 10 game days; one missed is in arrears for a game day, then the whole loan defaults). The autopilot borrows only to bridge (a) a new ship its own cash can't cover yet, when no ship it could build without a loan passes the buyer, against cash due from our cargo at sea that no loan counts on yet and for at most half the ship's cost and 7,500 Cr, or (b) a load at its source that cash limits (re-sizing at the source on; at most 7,500 Cr): when what the money earns over the loan's term (the new ship's net per hour, or the bigger load's extra cash profit) is at least 2× (setting) its interest, and it is earned back within half the term. The term is twice the payback plus the 2 game days it is repaid early, 1-360 game days. The case is weighed first at the Goldsmiths' published rate for the term (45% a year up to 30 game days, sliding to 30% at 360), then on the quote itself: `loan/quote` (balloon) against a lien on a ship of ours (at most 40% of her rebuild value and 7,500 Cr, recourse; every ship gets the same rate, so the first that quotes is taken, least likely lost first, up to 3 asked), its total repayable checked against the cap and the case, then `loan/accept-quote` at once under the cash lock (a quote stands 6 game hours). A lien goes only on a ship of ours that isn't insured (a strict read), listed for sale (every listing read), in a convoy or already pledged, least likely lost first (the piracy and storm losses of its lane; one at sea after those in port). **Refinancing:** for each Goldsmiths' loan of ours of 1,000 Cr or more, a request for quotes (`loan/request`: the same principal, balloon, to the old loan's maturity, a lien on another free ship of ours, with recourse, open 12 game hours) is posted once; a player's quote cheaper than the Goldsmiths' rate for those terms and than the old loan (never one of our companies'), saving at least 50 Cr over what is left of it, is approved (`loan/approve`) under the cash lock with every gate a new loan has (the count and cap aside: it replaces one loan with another), and the Goldsmiths' loan is repaid from it at once; a request nobody beats just lapses. What is owed, with interest, stays within 25% (setting) of net worth: the hourly leaderboard's figure for this company while fresh, else our own estimate (vaults and escrow, ships at their rebuild value, our goods aboard and in warehouses at base, less what we owe, plus what we are owed). At most 2 loans at once (a ship mortgage aside); none in the season's last 12 hours, while one is overdue, defaulted or has an installment in arrears, while a repayment is short, while our loans can't be read afresh or net worth hasn't been read in the last 15 minutes, while our own idle cash is quoted on a request (it is taken back first), or for 10 minutes after a lender refused. Repayment: what we owe (with interest to its repay date, and an amortised loan's installments as they fall due) is never spent on ships, marques, patrols, underwriting or lending; loads never spend what a repayment counts on unless cash due lands before it (it is an outflow on the cash timeline loads are planned on), not even a load's first call on its own plan's cash (its own load loan aside). The autopilot's own loans are repaid early from spare cash beyond what the fleet's loads are bound to buy, dearest annual rate first, whenever no ship is waiting for cash (remitting to the loan's region only when the interest saved beats the fee); every loan of ours, yours included, is repaid in full 2 game days before it matures (one of yours with a shorter term, half its term before), an installment in arrears at once, a defaulted loan's claim at once (paying it frees the arrested ship) and everything as the season ends, remitting from every region's spare; if cash is short then, no new loads, ships, marques or lending until it is paid. Loans from before loans by quote keep their rate a game day to maturity. The autopilot never walks away from a loan (`loan/default`: only by hand on the loans page). **The risk on a Goldsmiths' instant loan** (every loan the autopilot takes but a ship mortgage) is harsher than on a player's: a default (at maturity, a missed installment, a walk-away or bankruptcy) hands the pledged ship to them credited only at her break-up value, 35% of her rebuild value, with no surplus, and whatever is owed above that stays owed with recourse (taken from our vaults until paid; a ship sunk or lost first credits nothing). Player lenders and Goldsmiths' ship mortgages keep the 80% credit and pay the surplus. After any walk-away (any lender, by us or one of our own or linked companies) the Goldsmiths quote and lend nothing for a real day (`403 goldsmiths_walk_away_ban`): the autopilot then borrows nothing until the time the refusal gives (a real day if it gives none), logs it once and saves it; refinancing a loan already owed through a player lender goes on. A loan, approval, request or quote call with no answer is saved before it is sent and reconciled (a loan by its quote's id from `loan/list`, a request from our own requests, a quote from its request; given up only on a read 10 minutes after; until then a loan counts as owed), never sent again. Our loans (owed and made) are read every minute while any are out, and at once after a loan event (quote received, approved or released, foreclosure surplus, insurance paid to the lender, a walk-away included); the panel shows what is owed against the cap, each loan's annual rate, next payment, arrears, repay date, purpose and pledge, our requests and the quotes on them, and the interest paid so far.
- **Ship mortgages** (setting, on by default; only with leverage on; guide 3.4). When our own cash can't fund a turnkey build the buyer wants, the yard region's Goldsmiths may pay 60% of its price (80% for an oceanic hull), amortised over 180 game days with an installment every 10 at their rate, a lien on the new hull with recourse, one at a time. It is weighed beside an instant loan (the one cheaper in interest wins) and taken only within the debt cap (principal and interest), when the hull's net covers its installments 1.5× over and earns `minLoanReturnX` its interest over the term, and pays back our own part and the interest within the payback limit; our vault pays only the price less the mortgage. A mortgage is never repaid early or refinanced: only its next two installments are kept back from other spending, and it is repaid in full as the season ends. A default hands the ship to the Goldsmiths credited at 80% of her rebuild value, the surplus paid back. A mortgaged ship is never pledged, listed or broken up.
- **Idle cash** (setting, on by default). Spare cash beyond the fleet's loads, what our loans will take and 25,000 Cr, once it has had nothing to fund for 20 game hours (no ship waiting for cash, no load cut to the cash at its source), is quoted on players' open loan requests (`loan/requests` in the two regions with the most spare, `loan/quote-request`; the principal is escrowed from the vault there, no remittance): only a ship lien or warehouse goods (worth the principal at base), with recourse, 500 Cr up to half of what may be lent, at most 60 game days and maturing before the season's endgame, open at least another game hour, never one of our own companies' (nor one we already quoted), from a borrower whose request record shows no default or walk-away and whose credit record (`loan/credit`) is clean: no default ever, walk-away, write-off, open futures claim, bankruptcy or smuggling conviction. At most 4 quotes out, one a pass, the best expected interest first, at 0.9× the Goldsmiths' rate for the request's term, never under 15% a year, within its bounds. A quote is taken back (`loan/withdraw-quote`) when a ship waits for cash, the loads bound to buy need more than is spare or a load at its source was cut to the cash there in the last 10 minutes, while we owe on a loan, near the season's end, or when the spare falls under half what is quoted; otherwise it stands until the borrower approves it (a loan we made) or another quote, or the request lapses (its escrow comes back). Requests that can't be read don't stop quotes being taken back, but nothing new is quoted that pass; a quote's size is checked again under the cash lock. Only quotes the autopilot made are touched. A borrower's repayments, defaults and walk-aways are logged; a defaulted borrower's pledged ship comes to us and is taken command of at the next ship scan, pledged goods land in our warehouse there. Bank deposits are never made (a bank's insurance covers 2,500 Cr per player only).
- **Ship deals** (setting, on by default). Ships for sale (`fleet/ship-listings`, 50-150% of a hull's rebuild value) are read at every port where a ship of ours or a factor gives quay presence, each time the buyer looks. A listing is weighed against building her class (that yard's quote this pass, turnkey or own materials, else its last quote there, else the cheaper of the cheapest build seen and, if she was listed in the last 30 minutes, the listing's own rebuild value; her guns at 150 Cr each): one priced under that is judged like a build, from the stores she comes with (allowed classes and phase, max ships, cash after the fleet's loads and what our loans will take to repay, the upkeep floor she adds, payback within the limit on her first lane, the season), and the quickest payback wins, built or bought. She is bought with `buy-ship` under the cash lock (paid in the port's region) and taken command of at the next ship scan; a refusal (sold, withdrawn, her price out of its band now) drops that listing for half an hour, and a purchase with no answer is watched for by her ship id, never bought blind again. Never with a loan, never on an AI build order, never one of our companies' listings, and our ships are listed for sale only when housekeeping retires one (below). **Commissions:** every minute the open commissions at yards where we have presence (`contract/list`, `contract_type: commission`) are judged on a fresh quote there: the price less our cheapest build (turnkey, or own materials when that saves at least 300 Cr; own materials only when the turnkey walk takes anything from players' asks, since a commission's turnkey build passes over the buyer's own asks at no capped price, and not taken at all when own materials aren't open there) less 5% of the collateral (the risk of missing the deadline) must reach 10% of the price and 1,000 Cr, the class be open in this phase, at least 3 game hours left, and the build plus the collateral be covered by spare cash after the fleet's loads, our loans' repayments and the builds of commissions we already owe (kept back from loads, ships, marques, underwriting and lending until delivered). The best one is accepted (`accept-commission`, collateral escrowed) and delivered at once in the same cash lock (`deliver-commission`, the price and the collateral come back); a failed build is tried again every 2 minutes while we have presence at the yard (a book that can't supply turnkey tries own materials next; own materials that can't all be had fall back to turnkey only with no player's ask in its walk and while it still pays), before the deadline (one not sure to be ours still, after a call with no answer, is built turnkey only with nothing bought ahead, or waits), and the log warns when the deadline nears with no presence there. Calls with no answer are saved before they are sent and reconciled from `contract/list` and the `commission_*` notices, never sent blind again. Never one of our companies' commissions; not in the season's last hours, nor while a loan repayment is short. The panel shows the listings and commissions seen with their verdicts, ours being built, and what deals earned.
- **Intelligence envelopes** (setting, on by default). At each port where we have quay presence, every 2 game hours, the news held there is sealed and listed (`envelope/post`, 24 game hours): `news` (the coffee-house board's port reports and the last day's sailing notices, as the port view read in the last 5 minutes shows them; sailings only when none of ours, by company or ship name, is among them) and `charters` (the port's own non-public charters, narrowed with `commodity_ids` to goods the fleet isn't carrying, planning, reserving or letting lapse, so a posting we work is never sold). A section is asked for only when the port view shows something for it (an empty one is refused); a refusal rests that port and kind for 2 game hours. Price: 15 Cr a port report, 20 Cr a charter, 5 Cr a sailing, halved for every 12 game hours of the news' age, 10-300 Cr. The one a fresh envelope supersedes, and any unsold after 12 game hours, is withdrawn (a fresh one goes up only once the old one is off the market), and a `charters` envelope as soon as one of its goods is worked by the fleet; at most 16 of ours open (the game allows 20). Our envelopes are read every minute (`envelope/mine`, seller), and sooner after an `envelope_sold` notice: each sale is booked once to the deal stats; a post with no answer is found there or given up after 10 minutes, never posted blind again. Envelopes are never bought.
- **Housekeeping** (two settings, both on by default; guide 3.4, 3.12). **Breaking up dead weight:** the yard where a ship lies buys her for 35% of her rebuild value, paid by its region's Crown, and net worth counts a hull at its build value, so a break-up gives 65% of it away at once. Every 10 minutes each ship is judged: one idle in port for 2 game days and 3 checks running that earned less than the upkeep she adds, with no lane from where she lies beating it, or (the fleet full, the buyer on) an out-of-date class whose slot a paying new hull would take, is broken up only when the upkeep saved to the season's end (a week ahead at most), less what she would earn, makes up the net worth given away. Never the last ship, a packet, a smuggler, one arrested or under an AI order, with components fitted, cargo, freight or passengers aboard, a lane, a policy, a lien or mortgage, outside shares, a sale listing or a venture; one at a time, and none in the season's endgame. Away from a yard she sails there empty (only when the passage costs less than the payout); with ship deals on she is listed for a day first at 60% of her rebuild value (a sale pays at least half and she sails on), then broken up. A Crown that can't pay (409) is left for a game day and another region's yard tried. **Moving the home port:** a move costs 2,500 Cr from the new home's vault, at most once a real day, and needs presence there (a ship in port or a factor), never a cove; upkeep, marques, patrols and R&D then follow the new home. The remit fees paid to keep home funded are tracked by donor region: when one region paid most of them over 3 game days, those fees to the season's end beat the move 1.5× over, that region's vault holds the fee above its own floor and we have presence there, the home moves to its hub with a shipyard (else the best port we are at), at most once in 3 real days. The Exchequer's top-ups of Crowns run low are logged and kept for the AI, without waking the planner. The panel's Housekeeping card shows the candidates with their case, the ships being retired and retired, and the home's case.
- **Smuggling, the Free Trade** (setting, **off** by default; guide 3.18). It needs the company key's `underworld` scope, which the dashboard never puts on a key until you press **Allow smuggling** on the Underworld page (the key is minted again with it, renewals keep it, **Withdraw smuggling** mints one without it and revokes the old one; a running autopilot switches at once). Without the scope the mode is unavailable, whatever the setting. One ship at a time, never the fleet's only one: the fastest in port with nothing lawful aboard or loaded, no sealed freight, not pledged to a loan, insured, listed for sale, sharing her ownership (ship shares listed or sold), in a convoy, arrested or under an AI order (each read fresh; a read that fails rules her out). With no smuggler out, the Crown's attention cool and the season normal, every minute it weighs each free ship (fastest first) × each open cove × each fresh want it knows: wants are read live (`underworld/wants`) wherever a ship of ours lies in an ordinary port or a factor sits, and from a cove's board when a ship of ours is there (the age of each report kept); an idle ship near a cove (600 nm) goes to read its board at most once a game day when no want is known. A want counts only fresh (a board's at most a game day old, our own sighting half a day for goods already aboard) and rich (3× base or more to buy for). A run is estimated on goods at about base (the book is read for the real price at the cove: the house ask and players' asks, at most 5% over the best), at most `smuggleMaxTons` (5) and `smuggleMaxPence` (3,000 Cr) and what the want still takes, the want faded for its age at landing (taken to lose 35% of its premium a game day and be gone after 3 game days, then the goods fetch half base back at a cove) and walked down by our own tons; the route from the cove only through sea zones with no patrol (`world/zones` `patrol_suppression_bps` = 0, guide 3.5: at sea only patrols search) and rated for her hull. **Value test:** expected gain = P(ok) × proceeds − goods − P(caught) × ship (her rebuild value and guns) − her time at her lawful rate must be positive and at least 2× the expected loss P(caught) × (ship + goods). P(caught) is a conservative model, learned and saved: 3% a run (a patrol funded on the route under way, what we can't see) and 1% a game hour lying in an ordinary port's roadstead (berthed hours count 3×: a free berth takes her on arrival for its half-hour lock; a run ashore takes ~4 game hours until our own runs show), moved by our own voyages, hours in port and seizures but never under half those priors, raised by half for each run ashore in the last 72 game hours (the Crown's attention), and the next run waits 12 game hours after one, doubling with each in that window (at most 96). At the cove she berths (a cove is never searched) and buys the stores the whole run needs first (there and back plus 3 days: none may be bought in an ordinary port with contraband aboard), then, her fitness checked again (no lien, listing, shares or policy since), the goods (IOC, under the cash lock, never what loads, loans or a ship waiting for cash count on), is judged again on what filled, and sails alone (never in a convoy) once the zones are read afresh and still clear: refused if any patrol now works the route. At the wanting port she leaves the berth the moment the lock lets her, reads the want and runs the goods ashore at once (`underworld/run`), waiting out `completes_at` in the roadstead (departing would cancel it); if the want is gone, on to the next fresh want her stores reach with enough left to go on from there to a cove clear of patrols (no stores may be bought with contraband aboard, so she is never stranded where she may be searched), else back to the nearest cove clear of patrols, where they are sold into players' bids at a fair price (what they cost, at least half base; a quarter after 6 game hours) or held for a want. Empty again, she goes back to lawful work. Every decision is logged and kept for the panel (wants seen, our smugglers, runs with cost, pay and outcome, the model's figures). A seizure (`ship_seized`, by ship id; or a ship gone from the fleet with contraband aboard, not sunk, taken by pirates, sold or broken up, and no notice why, placed at sea or in port only where her last read tells it) is counted once per ship (the ids are saved; the public `smuggler_seized` notice names ships by name only, which isn't unique, so it never counts one of ours: it only makes the fleet be read again) (the ship and everything aboard are forfeit, insurance pays nothing, a conviction goes on our public credit record), and the mode **switches itself off** (saved off) until you turn it on again (CLI: restart with `KIBB_SMUGGLING=resume`). Turned off meanwhile (by you, Claude or a seizure), nothing new is bought and no want elsewhere is sailed for; a run ashore under way finishes, and a load still aboard is finished before she is let go: run ashore at a live want where she lies, else taken to a cove clear of patrols and sold there into a fair bid (held at the cove until one comes). Only without the scope is it held where she lies. Elsewhere: contraband aboard (bought by hand too) is taken by the mode when it is on and the ship is fit; no auto-patrol or AI patrol goes where our smugglers sail or are about to (an AI patrol is checked again under the cash lock just before it is funded), nor anywhere while a ship with contraband aboard lies in an ordinary port with no passage planned, or holds a lane whose passage isn't known; a lawful ship with contraband aboard (bought by hand) never sails through a zone a patrol works (as last read; a zone not read counts) nor, in an ordinary port, short of the passage's safe stores (none may be bought there): she waits in the roadstead, logged once; underwriting never writes on our own ships; loans never pledge a smuggler or a ship with contraband aboard; convoys never include either.
- **Season end.** Once the operators announce it (`season_planned_end_at`; the panel shows it), only work that lands at least 30 minutes before the end is planned: legs, freight lots and leftovers carried elsewhere. A completion bonus counts only for a tier that can finish in time; no hull is built that can't pay back before the end, nor any in the last 12 hours, and no marques then either; in the last 3 hours goods are never sold under base price (they count at base in net worth); a stalled posting whose re-post would come after the end is delivered to.
- **World pause and notices.** While the operators pause the world (`world_paused`), ships wait without trying anything, housekeeping only reads the clock every 30 s, and nothing is bought, sold, remitted or built; on resume the world is read again and every ship looks again. The panel and header show the pause. A critical server announcement shows as a banner on the panel, and a header badge, for a day.
- **AI support (Claude)** (off by default; switch it Off / Shadow / Live on the fleet panel, needs `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` in `.env.local`). Claude acts as strategist (Opus 5.5 by default, or Sonnet 5.5 at half the price; low thinking effort by default, which is most of a call's cost): every 60 minutes by default (half that near a phase turnover; a routine call is skipped when nothing important changed), and soon after a phase change, a stall escalation, a lost ship or a trip far below its estimate, it reads a digest of the fleet and the world (each ship's guns, stores and cash for its next load; sea zones with their piracy, rival traffic and our exposure; yards with their last quotes and the buyer's best options; the budgets; every setting with the range it may set it to; rivals due at open charters; and its own directives in force with what became of them) and answers with directives. Live, they apply until they lapse (15-180 min), and every autopilot check still runs on them:
  - **Settings:** any setting but its own (its mode, caps, model, effort and cadence can't even be named), within the range the digest shows (spend ceilings yours, the reserve only raised), under hard floors applied wherever settings are read: max ships, the marque budget, the underwriting budgets, the debt cap (`maxDebtPct`) and the cash due counted (`cashDuePct`) never above yours, the home reserve and a loan's required return (`minLoanReturnX`, up to 4× yours) only ever raised (the home floor still covers 1.5 days of bills), no ship buying without the key's `infra_manage`. It may switch ship deals and envelopes either way, and leverage, ship mortgages, idle-cash lending, passengers, break-ups, moving home and smuggling off: never on while yours are off (it may also lower `smuggleMaxTons` and `smuggleMaxPence`, never raise them). The digest's Free Trade section shows the mode's state, the wants seen, our smugglers, runs and seizures. The digest shows what we owe against the cap, each loan (annual rate, repayment, next payment and arrears, repay date, a hull or a load), what borrowers owe us, our idle-cash quotes, our requests for quotes with the best quote on each, and the Goldsmiths' rate by term. A setting you change wins at once for the rest of its advice.
  - **Bans and bias:** bans on ports (no loading, delivering or selling there), sea zones (no passage, laden or empty), goods, charters, or public charters rivals are due at first; and a bias of 0.5-1.5x on lane ranks by either end, zone, good, charter or lane (only lanes that already pass every check). Bans apply to new plans only: cargo aboard is never stranded.
  - **Stall calls** on stalled expansion charters (let it lapse for the re-post near the end, or deliver now).
  - **Ship orders,** one per ship: take a given lane or fill a given charter at its next plan (when one passes every check; else it plans as usual), go to a port (loads from there only, or sailing there empty over a rated route no ban rules out), hold in the roadstead (at most a third of the fleet; held ships count as idle for the tripwire), or drop a planned lane nothing was bought for. A policy you bought, sealed freight and cargo aboard always come first.
  - **The buyer:** hold, forbid classes, or one build of a given class at a given yard through all the buyer's checks (payback limit, cash after loads, max ships, the season); it needs a ship of ours there, or a factor with yardFactor on.
  - **Marques and patrols** (at most 2 an answer): checked against the zone's cap and the season, priced by the game's quote for the zone (`marque-quote` / `patrol-quote`, again just before issuing), never a patrol where a ship of ours carries contraband, paid from spare cash after the fleet's loads (never by remitting), within what the marque budget (yours, shared with auto-marques) has left over the last 24 hours, for a term no longer than its advice. An issued one can't be withdrawn: it runs its term even when the advice is cleared. Set the marque budget to 0 to forbid them.
  - **Money orders,** through every check of the autopilot's own: borrow up to a sum for a given ship's next load (at its source, when cash limits the load; leverage and re-sizing on) or for a new ship the buyer can't fund with its own cash (not only against cash due), within the debt cap and the required return and payback; repay a given loan early in full from spare cash after the fleet's loads (never while a ship waits for cash); build a given commission to order (even with ship deals off; every commission check applies). The digest lists our loans (D1…) and the commissions at our yards (K1…) by alias.
  - **Housekeeping orders:** break up a given ship through every check the dead-weight path makes but its days of watching (every never, one at a time, a quoted rebuild value, a yard in reach and the value test still apply; refused while break-ups are off), carried out at her own turns like any mark; or move the home port to a given port, the fee's case its call (its reason logged beside the autopilot's own), with presence there, never a cove, that vault paying the fee above its floor and 3 real days between moves still required. The digest shows passengers (fares, aboard, offers, what the pricer learned), the buyer's ship-mortgage checks, dead-weight candidates with their case, the home's remit fees by region and its verdict, and the Crowns topped up.

  What can't be done is refused and comes back to it as a fixed status code (never the game's own wording); every outcome is logged. Shadow logs the same answers without applying them. Ships, charters, lanes and rivals appear to it only as aliases, and game notices only as fields Claude Haiku 5.5 extracted, so nothing other players write reaches it. Spend is capped per UTC day and in total ($2 and $180 by default; a call whose worst case could pass a cap is skipped); half the fleet idle or held under its directives for 20 minutes clears them (marques and patrols already issued run on); if trips under its advice earn under 80% of trips without, it drops back to shadow. Every decision is kept in MongoDB (`ai_decisions`); the dashboard shows the spend, every directive in force with its reason and status, and why. The planner also learns three figures on its own: how often a stalled posting stays stalled to its lapse, and how often rivals break a stall, per charter, and how fast remembered bids drift; and it discounts charters by what rivals due there first are likely to deliver.
- **Buying ships.** Wherever the fleet has presence at a shipyard (a ship there, or a Resident Factor) it quotes every allowed class, scores each by payback hours on its best lane net of the extra fleet upkeep (an oceanic hull adds half its tons to sprawl as well as 1 Cr a ton), builds the quickest payback within your limit (unarmed unless guns are on "full"). With no presence at any yard, it prices a build at the yard nearest the fleet's work from the last quotes it saw; if that pays back (fee included) it hires a Resident Factor there (750 Cr), builds, and dismisses the factor straight after, so building never waits for a ship to pass by and no factor salary runs on (setting, on by default; never past the company's 12 factors). A new hull's cargo room counts the stores its first lane really needs (a big crew on a long passage takes several tons). Turnkey buys each material off the yard port's ask book (players' asks and the NPC ladder, cheapest first), pays customs on every fill and the yard's 15% markup on the NPC-sourced part only. When building from its own materials saves at least 300 Cr (that markup) it does so instead (setting, on by default): it buys what the yard's quote lists on that port's own market into a warehouse lease there (IOC bids capped a little over the quote's per-ton price, customs paid), builds with `byom`, and shrinks the lease back after; materials it couldn't finish buying stay in the warehouse for the next try. A turnkey build the book can't supply (409) drops that yard's cached quote. Under a royal monopoly on a good (another company holds the Crown's asks in a banking region, read with the world and from the public notices), lanes source it there only from players' resting asks, and a buy the monopoly refuses is noted and planned around rather than retried. A packet (the 16 t courier that can seal its dispatches) is never built, whatever the class list says. New ships are named after random Discord bots from the [TopStats](https://topstats.gg) API (set `TOPSTATS_API_KEY`), or a bundled list without a key.

Settings (cash reserve, minimum margin, max ships, max payback, allowed classes, guns, phase-bonus weight, storm losses counted, freight, yard factor, own-materials builds, rival intel, let stalled postings lapse, always hold for re-post, world events, cash kept back near a new phase, re-sizing loads at the source, cash due counted, marques and underwriting with their budgets, leverage with its debt cap and required return, ship mortgages, idle-cash lending, passengers, ship deals, envelopes, breaking up dead weight, moving the home port, smuggling with its limits) apply live; the dashboard sends only the fields you changed. The panel also shows where each ship's time goes (laden, empty at sea, in port, idle), its Cr/hour, and each finished trip's planner estimate against what it made. The home-region reserve never drops below 1.5 game days of fleet upkeep and the wealth levy (sprawl on coastal and regional tons plus half the oceanic tons, factors, and 1 Cr per oceanic ton; or what the ledger shows was billed if more), whatever the setting, and every other region keeps 200 Cr for dues and stores; each region also keeps 1.5 days of its warehouse rent, and stores to sail safely may dip into all of that. Upkeep is billed from home, so every 10 minutes the autopilot tops the home vault up to its floor from the other regions' spare (the home port is read again then, and at once on `home_port_relocated`, so a move on the company page is followed: floor, marques and patrols switch to the new home's region). Auto-remit for bills (the company page) is only read and reported, never changed: off, the log says a region that can't cover its own bill meets the bailiff. The enabled flag persists, so a running autopilot resumes when the server restarts, and so does its progress: each ship's lane and whether its cargo is aboard, its sell floors, trips, stats, purchases, the log, the event cursor and what the world model had learned (prices, stalled charters, tier pace, rival intel: sightings, public policies and convoys, rival deliveries per charter (from the expansion board), net-worth history; per expansion charter, how often rivals broke its stalls and our own flow into it; the postings being let lapse; world events, and what they did to prices and crisis charters; the storm record), the count of loads re-sized at their source, and our loans (what each was taken for, credit calls with no answer still to reconcile, our idle-cash quotes and our requests for quotes; an older save's board offers are dropped and its loans kept on their daily rate), and the smuggling mode (the learned seizure model, our smugglers mid-run, the runs, the Crown's attention, the halt after a seizure, convictions, the wants seen, its decisions). That is saved to MongoDB when `MONGODB_URI` is set (`fleet_snapshots`, one document per company, and `fleet_trips`, every finished trip), and always to `.kibb/fleet-<company>.json`; on start the newer copy wins. Trips, loads, deliveries and buyer actions save at once, everything else at most every 15 s. On SIGINT/SIGTERM the server saves before exiting when run with `NEXT_MANUAL_SIG_HANDLE=true` (the pm2 config sets it). A lease file in `.kibb/` stops a second process on this machine (another server or the CLI) from running the same company; with `MONGODB_URI` set a lease document (`fleet_leases`) does it across machines too (while the database can't be reached, the file alone holds, and start-up never waits on it). Don't give manual orders to ships while it runs.

To run it without the dashboard (e.g. on a server):

```bash
pnpm exec tsx --env-file=.env.local src/workers/kibb-fleet.ts
```

with `KIBB_API_KEY` (a company-bound key) in `.env.local`. See the header of [src/workers/kibb-fleet.ts](src/workers/kibb-fleet.ts) for its settings. Mind the key's lifetime: `POST /v1/auth/login` keys last 24 h unless you pass `ttl_seconds` (up to 31,536,000, a year), and `create-key` with handle and passphrase and no `ttl_seconds` never expires. The autopilot stops when its key expires; it warns in the log at 7 days, 1 day and 1 hour left (from `whoami.expires_at`; each once, restarts included). In the dashboard the company key is renewed by itself in its last 7 days while you are signed in (the running autopilot switches to the new key without a restart); the panel warns in that last week and the header in the last 3 days, and an autopilot that couldn't be resumed after a server restart says why on the panel.

## Project Structure

```
src/
├── app/
│   ├── (auth)/              # Sign-in, register, company picker
│   ├── (game)/              # Every game page (see Pages)
│   └── api/kibb/            # Session routes, autopilot control, and /v1 proxy to the game API
├── components/kibb/         # Shared panels, tables, badges
├── instrumentation.ts       # Resumes enabled autopilots on server start
├── lib/kibb/
│   ├── client.ts            # Typed API client (Idempotency-Key, retries, 429 handling)
│   ├── fleet/               # Autopilot: world model, lane planner, ship agents, manager, ship names
│   ├── fleet-registry.ts    # In-process autopilots, one per company
│   ├── store.ts             # .kibb/store.json
│   ├── schema.ts            # Every API schema, generated: node scripts/gen-kibb-schema.mjs
│   └── types.ts             # Hand-written API types used by the autopilot
└── workers/kibb-fleet.ts    # CLI for the autopilot
```

The browser never sees a company key: `/api/kibb/v1/*` forwards to the game with the active company's stored key, and refuses key management, company founding and bankruptcy. Those go through dedicated routes: account actions use the signed-in player's key, changing company settings mints a two-minute `account_manage` key and revokes it straight after, and bankruptcy needs the company's name typed back and stops the autopilot first. Allowing or withdrawing smuggling (`/api/kibb/company/underworld`) mints the company key again with or without the `underworld` scope using the signed-in player's key, switches a running autopilot to it and revokes the key it replaces; the store records which scopes each company key holds, and renewals keep them. Passphrases are passed through, never stored. It, and the autopilot route, act only for a signed-in player who owns the selected company (checked against the game, cached for 5 minutes); the company cookie alone does nothing.
